\section{Experiments and Benchmark Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

In this section, we present comprehensive empirical evaluations of the proposed \textbf{GDF-Net (FANet-MFAD)} across seven benchmark biomedical segmentation datasets. We conduct extensive cross-domain comparisons against the original FANet baseline, perform forensic audits of published literature anomalies, and execute ablation studies validating the necessity of each architectural component.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Experimental Setup and Benchmark Datasets}
We evaluate our framework across four diverse clinical and biological imaging domains encompassing seven standard datasets:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{CVC-ClinicDB~\cite{bernal2017comparative}:} 612 colonoscopy frames extracted from 29 video sequences showing mucosal polyps (resolution: $256 \times 256$, split: 551 train, 61 test).
  \item \textbf{EM-Dataset~\cite{cardona2010integrated}:} 30 serial transmission electron microscopy sections of the \textit{Drosophila} ventral nerve cord displaying cellular membranes (resolution: $512 \times 512$, split: 27 train, 3 test).
  \item \textbf{Kvasir-SEG~\cite{jha2020kvasir}:} 1{,}000 clinically validated gastrointestinal polyp images from Oslo University Hospital (resolution: $512 \times 512$, split: 880 train, 120 test).
  \item \textbf{ISIC-2018~\cite{codella2019skin}:} 2{,}594 high-resolution dermoscopic images from the International Skin Imaging Collaboration challenge for melanoma lesion boundary segmentation (resolution: $256 \times 256$, split: 2{,}334 train, 260 test).
  \item \textbf{CHASE-DB1~\cite{hoover2000locating}:} 28 multi-ethnic retinal fundus photographs from 14 school children for retinal blood vessel segmentation (resolution: $512 \times 512$, split: 20 train, 8 test).
  \item \textbf{DRIVE~\cite{staal2004ridge}:} 40 retinal photographs for vessel tree extraction (resolution: $512 \times 512$, split: 20 train, 20 test). In our benchmark, we utilize knowledge transfer from the ISIC-2018 pre-trained backbone with $\times 32$ data repeat factors to prevent representational collapse in this low-sample regime.
  \item \textbf{DSB-2018~\cite{caicedo2019nucleus}:} 670 multi-source fluorescent and histology microscopy images from the 2018 Data Science Bowl for nucleus segmentation (resolution: $256 \times 256$, split: 536 train, 134 test). Evaluated using an In-RAM preloading pipeline ensuring high-throughput iteration.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Data Augmentation and Training Protocols}
To ensure rigorous and reproducible comparability, data augmentation adheres strictly to the canonical protocols specified in Table~I of the original FANet paper~\cite{tomar2021fanet}: horizontal flipping ($p=0.5$), vertical flipping ($p=0.5$), random rotation ($\pm 45^\circ$, $p=0.6$), affine shift and scaling, elastic distortions ($p=0.3$), grid distortions ($p=0.3$), and random brightness/contrast adjustment ($p=0.3$). 

Networks are trained using the Adam optimizer with initial learning rate $\eta = 10^{-4}$ and an adaptive schedule ($\text{ReduceLROnPlateau}$, factor $0.5$, patience $5$). Training is conducted for 100 epochs (200 epochs for CVC-ClinicDB) with automatic mixed precision ($\text{fp16}$) on NVIDIA Tesla T4 and A100 GPUs. Evaluation is executed in the final feedback phase ($t=2$) without test-time augmentation.

\subsubsection{Evaluation Metrics}
Segmentation performance is quantified using six standard metrics: Dice Similarity Coefficient (Dice), mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU), Sensitivity/Recall, Specificity, Precision, and False Positive Rate ($\text{FPR} = \frac{\text{FP}}{\text{FP} + \text{TN}}$). Metrics are computed on individual test samples and aggregated as mean $\pm$ standard deviation.

\subsection{Forensic Audit: Resolving Literature Discrepancies and Dormant Feedback}
A critical scientific inquiry in recurrent feedback learning concerns the validity and integrity of previously reported benchmark tables. In Table~IX of the seminal FANet paper~\cite{tomar2021fanet}, the authors reported feedback ablation results comparing feedforward passes ($B_1$) against multiple feedback iterations ($B_2 \to B_4$). A forensic audit of this table reveals a startling mathematical phenomenon, summarized in Table~\ref{tab:paper_discrepancy}:

\begin{table}[h!]
\centering
\caption{Forensic audit of Table IX in Tomar et al.~\cite{tomar2021fanet}. Across multiple cohorts, reported scores are bit-for-bit identical from $B_1$ through $B_4$, demonstrating that the original feedback mechanism was effectively dormant.}
\label{tab:paper_discrepancy}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular*}{\columnwidth}{@{\extracolsep{\fill}}l c c c c r@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Dataset} & \textbf{Metric} & \textbf{$B_1$} & \textbf{$B_2$} & \textbf{$B_4$} & \textbf{Reported $\Delta$} \\
\midrule
Kvasir-SEG       & mDice  & 0.8803 & 0.8803 & 0.8803 & \textbf{0.0000} \\
CVC-ColonDB      & mDice  & 0.7788 & 0.7788 & 0.7788 & \textbf{0.0000} \\
ETIS-Larib       & mDice  & 0.6865 & 0.6865 & 0.6865 & \textbf{0.0000} \\
\midrule
\textbf{Kvasir-SEG (Ours)} & mDice & 0.8712 & \textbf{0.8880} & 0.8882 & \textbf{+1.68\,pp} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular*}
\end{table}

\textbf{Diagnosis:}
The exact numerical identity $B_1 \equiv B_4$ across three independent cohorts reveals that the feedback branch in the original codebase was effectively \textit{dormant} during evaluation (producing zero iterative delta). Conversely, when previous researchers attempted to force feedback learning by training for prolonged cycles (up to 500 epochs), the unconstrained gradient flow through the feedback branch triggered severe over-segmentation and representational collapse.

In contrast, under the Feedback Firewall principle, GDF-Net produces active, genuine boundary refinement between $t=1$ and $t=2$ (+1.68\,pp improvement on Kvasir-SEG) while converging stably within 100 epochs, proving that gradient decoupling is the essential prerequisite for activating meaningful recurrent visual processing.

\subsection{Ablation Studies and Mechanistic Disentanglement}
To isolate the contribution of each architectural innovation, we conduct systematic ablation experiments summarized in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}.

\begin{table}[h!]
\centering
\caption{Ablation analysis on CVC-ClinicDB ($N=61$) and ISIC-2018 ($N=260$). Removing gradient decoupling ($\text{detach}$) results in severe performance degradation and false-positive explosion.}
\label{tab:ablation}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular*}{\columnwidth}{@{\extracolsep{\fill}}l c c c c@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Configuration} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{\textbf{CVC-ClinicDB}} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{\textbf{ISIC-2018}} \\
\cmidrule(lr){2-3} \cmidrule(lr){4-5}
& \textbf{Dice} & \textbf{FPR (\%)} & \textbf{Dice} & \textbf{FPR (\%)} \\
\midrule
(A) Feedforward Baseline (No FB)     & 0.8958 & 0.97\% & 0.8731 & 3.48\% \\
(B) Canonical Feedback (No Detach)   & 0.8812 & 3.12\% & 0.8624 & 5.19\% \\
(C) GDF-Net w/ Single-Scale Decoder  & 0.9245 & 0.68\% & 0.8872 & 1.84\% \\
(D) \textbf{Full GDF-Net (Proposed)} & \textbf{0.9427} & \textbf{0.40\%} & \textbf{0.9000} & \textbf{1.32\%} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular*}
\end{table}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Impact of Gradient Decoupling (B vs D):} Retaining the recursive gradient graph (configuration B) causes the network to collapse into the Feedback Trap: FPR balloons by over $7\times$ (from 0.40\% to 3.12\%), and Dice drops by $-6.15$\,pp. Introducing the Feedback Firewall ($\text{detach}(m)$) restores optimal convergence.
  \item \textbf{Multi-Scale Decoder Modulation (C vs D):} Confining feedback modulation to a single layer yields 0.9245 Dice on CVC-ClinicDB. Extending modulation hierarchically across all five decoder levels in MFAD delivers an additional $+1.82$\,pp improvement, confirming that multi-scale spatial priors are necessary to resolve both coarse semantic context and fine mucosal contours.
  \item \textbf{Transfer Learning in Low-Sample Regimes (DRIVE):} On the 20-sample DRIVE benchmark, training from scratch produced representational collapse (0.2837 Dice). Transferring the pre-trained ISIC-2018 encoder backbone elevated performance to \textbf{0.7932} Dice (+3.14\,pp over baseline), establishing that pre-trained feature representations prevent overfitting in extreme low-data biomedical domains.
\end{itemize}
